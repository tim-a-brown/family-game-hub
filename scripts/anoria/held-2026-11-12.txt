The five held cards and the Walk to Canossa (2026-11-12)
=========================================================

Tim: "finish the unfinished stuff", and he accepted "a card that switches off an enemy Figure's skill, like Guard or
Steadfast". set-150.json held five cards of the third printing back for want of an effect (needsEngine; they are
needsEngineBuilt now, with what was built). Each has an effect of its own, found only in packs (pr 3, in no ready deck):

  Damascus Steel           Relic, 2   Your Figures have +1 Might when attacking.                       (as written)
  Abacus                   Relic, 2   Your Deeds cost 1 less.                                          (as written)
  The Peace of Westphalia  Deed S, 1  Choose a Domain. Nobody can start a clash there until this round ends.
  The Lisbon Earthquake    Deed W, 1  Choose a Domain. Every Figure there, yours and the other player's, misses its
                                      next turn.                                                       (as written)
  The Edict of Milan       Deed S, 2  Choose a Domain where you have a Figure. Until this round ends, the other player
                                      can't start a clash with your Figures there.
  The Walk to Canossa      Deed S, 1  Choose an enemy Figure. Until this round ends, the skills and powers on its card
                                      don't work.                                                      (new: Henry IV, 1077)

Adapted to today's rules: "one Place" is a Domain. The Peace and the Edict lie on the Domain until the count (S.dip,
as the Roman Triumph does), so the protection belongs to the position: a Figure that walks in is covered and one that
walks out is not. Canossa lies on its Figure (f.hush) and lifts at the count, after the count's own tests (a humbled
Shakespeare scores no Stage that round).

The engine
----------
  kOf(S, f)      every test of a Figure's skill on the table (guard, steadfast, law, ward, untouchable, swift, voyager,
                 monument, inspiring, uprising, cynic and the rest) reads it here, not from the card, so Canossa takes
                 them all away at once: a humbled Diogenes's Silence lifts, a humbled Imhotep can be moved, a humbled
                 Leo gives no Ward. A card not yet in play (costs, scholar on arrival) is read from the card.
  noClash        the Peace of Westphalia (truceOn): nobody starts a clash there, Ambush included
  clashable      the Edict of Milan (edictOn) and untouchable: who an enemy may start a clash with
  might          Damascus Steel: +1 in mode 1 (attacking)
  costOf         Abacus: Deeds 1 less, adding with Ada Lovelace / Al-Khwarizmi and Ironbridge, never below 0 (a
                 Tribune's Veto then costs 0 and plays itself with no Spark kept)
  Ward and the Forbidden City keep the other player's Peace, Earthquake and Canossa off their Figures. The Tribune's
  Veto cancels the Lisbon Earthquake where it would stop one of your Figures (as it does Eclipse), and not the Peace,
  the Edict or Canossa (none moves, stuns or knocks out your Figures).
  New events: quake {o, c, p, us} (its stun events follow), hush {u, c, o}. Each has its EV moment, its logEvs
  sentence and its moveSaid sentence.
  The robots (HW in engine.js): dipWorth counts a Peace at TRUCE_W 0.5 of the Domain's worth to whoever holds it, and
  an Edict at 0.5 of it (0.3 while its player doesn't hold it) plus 0.06 for each Might it shelters, only while the
  other player still has a turn this round and has Figures there; quakeWorth counts 0.12 a Might plus 0.25 a Figure
  stopped, for or against; Canossa looks one clash ahead, as Florence's loan does. All are 0 unless one of the six is
  in play, so the ready decks' games are untouched.

On the table
------------
  Damascus Steel comes into a clash as its card under the attacker, as Phalanx does, with its +1 (aid(); its name
  plate keeps clear of the number). The Abacus's slot bumps when its player's Deed is played. The Peace and the Edict
  are stamped onto the Domain (a disc in the player's colour) and stay as its tile mark until the count: sheathed
  swords under a bar, a shield; the Edict's Figures hop. The Lisbon Earthquake shakes the Domain's row, then each
  Figure it stops is marked. Canossa: the piece's first skill mark, struck through in red on a dark disc at its top
  left, the piece dimmed, the card's powers struck through when it is read, and a sentence under it saying so.
  Played with the page's test hook at 402x874, 874x402, 768x1024 and 320x568 (scratchpad held5/moments.js and
  games.js): every moment filmed, whole games with the six on top of both draw piles at each size, no page errors,
  nothing clipped (the one hit, .ll-sr, is the Legacy Line's screen-reader text, hidden on purpose).

Sources and pictures
--------------------
set-150.json has each card's src and check. Read in full on archive.org: Lactantius, Of the Manner in Which the
Persecutors Died, ch. 45 and 48 (the Milan meeting, Licinius's wedding there, the letter of 13 June 313 and the
restoring of the Christians' places); Henderson, Select Historical Documents of the Middle Ages (Gregory VII's
deposition of Henry IV, "I absolve all Christians from the bonds of the oath", and Gregory's own letter on Canossa:
"with bare feet and clad in wool, he continued for three days to stand before the gate of the castle"). From web
search only: the Lisbon earthquake (date, wave, five days of fire, Pombal's grid and quake-proof buildings), the
Peace of Westphalia and ter Borch's painting, Damascus steel (sure: false) and Cheng Dawei's Suanfa tongzong of 1592
(sure: false; the number of chapters differs between sources, so the card leaves it out).
Legacy Lines: Damascus Steel from Damascus (the Domain), the Lisbon Earthquake from Lisbon, the Edict of Milan from
Constantine. The Abacus, the Peace and Canossa have none: no card in the set is part of their history.
