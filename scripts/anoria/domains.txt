ANORIA: DOMAINS ON THE GAME'S OWN ENGINE. Test games, 2026-11-11.
node scripts/anoria/domains.js <experiment>, on games/anoria/engine.js as it now is. The prototype's tables are in
places/results.txt; these ask the same questions of the real rules. A game with no stacks plays exactly as the engine
did before Domains: 1,275 games (the ready decks in every style, random decks from the whole set, the old doubled
scoring) gave the same winner, rounds, Renown, Seals and knock-outs, game for game.

HOW THE GAMES WERE PLAYED
  First to 25 Renown or three Seals. A cell is the five ready decks (40 cards) against each other, all 20 pairings,
  both seats, each deck in its own style and with its own ready stack of 15 Domains. Set-up is played: each robot
  draws three, lays two and offers the third, the dice decide whose offer is the Crossroads, the other player goes
  first. Noise: one deck's win rate is good to about 2.5 points on 1,000 games (400 for the deck), 1.25 on 4,000 and
  0.9 on 8,000. A spread between the five decks under 4 on 4,000 games is "level".
  The machine was shared and slow, so the samples are smaller than the prototype's. Where a number rests on few
  games it says so.

WHAT IS NOT SETTLED
  - The ready decks sit 3.8 apart over 12,000 games, with Pathfinders about 2 over and Explorers about 2 under. One
    batch of 4,000 read 7.4. The gap is the decks' own, and a change to a deck's cards, not its stack, is what
    would close it.
  - A defensive lay at set-up (the Domain your Figures are most at home on at your own Gate, never offered; Routes
    off your Gate) is about 2 points better than laying forward, cuts Seal wins from 33% to 29% and costs the raiding
    decks 3 to 4 points. The robots lay forward and the stacks are balanced for that. People will lay as they like.
  - A stack of fifteen Domains from one home gives a one-region deck about 10 points (Legacy 61%).
  - Land's fort rule is about 2 points under every other rule.
  - The Guides were measured on 200 games a deck.
  - The Domain cards are costed in random decks at about 500 to 1,000 draws each: good to about 2 points.
  - Three or more players: the engine's set-up and Domain cards are written for N players where that was free, but
    The Columbian Exchange is for two, and nothing with Domains was played with more than two.
