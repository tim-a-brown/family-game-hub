Anoria for three to six players, and in teams (2026-11-13)
==========================================================

Tim, 2026-11-11: "i originally wanted anoria to be playable for up to 6 players -- either everyone for themselves or in
teams of up to two or three people. Would it be possible to adapt the map such that each new player is a spoke off of
the crossroads?" And 2026-11-13: "start on 3 to 6 players and teams".

This is the rules engine side (games/anoria/engine.js and the scripts here). The table on the page is being built
separately; the page still plays two players, exactly as before.

How these were measured: node scripts/anoria/players.js <shape> [games] [seed] ['<json>']. The five ready decks are
dealt round the table in a fresh order every game (six players: one deck twice), each with the robot style
endings.js gives it (Explorers and Pathfinders raid, Legion brawls, Legacy keeps, Court holds), with Domains, on the
game's rules (STD). Seats are counted from the player who took the first turn of the game (seat 1) round the table to
the left; in a team game, each team, from the first player's team. 600 games a line unless it says otherwise: a seat's
share moves by about 2 points between two sets of 600 games, so a spread of 4 or 5 is noise.


1. What the engine does now (the rules, as a player would read them)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
The map. Each player's Gate and Outpost are a spoke off the Crossroads; with three or more players, each Outpost is
also joined to the Outposts on either side. Set-up is as for two: everyone draws four Domains, lays one at their Gate
and one at their Outpost, offers one for the Crossroads and puts the last under their stack. Everyone rolls a die; on a
tie for the highest roll, only the players in the tie roll again. The highest roll's offer goes to the Crossroads, the
other offers go to their players' discard piles, and the player on the left of the highest roll takes the first turn.

Turns. Each round the side with the most Renown goes first and play goes round the table to the left (level: it goes
round by rounds from whoever took the first turn of the game). Greenwich's holder still takes the last turn. In a
team game the leading team's players take that first turn by rounds.

Seals and being put out. Holding a player's Gate when a round ends breaks one of their Seals, as now. A player with no
Seals left is out of the game: their Figures leave the map for their discard pile, they take no more turns and can't
win. The Domains they brought stay where they lie, with their banners; their Gate and Outpost are Domains like any
other (anyone can hold them and they score, and holding them breaks nothing, since there is no Seal left). With two
players this is exactly today's Seal ending. The game ends when one side is left (it wins), or at the count where a
side reaches the target (of the sides still in, the most Renown wins).

Teams. Teammates sit on alternating spokes (2 against 2: seats 1 and 3 against 2 and 4; 3 against 3: 1, 3, 5 against
2, 4, 6; three teams of two: 1 and 4, 2 and 5, 3 and 6). Teammates can't clash with each other, nothing that hits an
enemy hits a teammate, and holding a teammate's Gate breaks none of their Seals. Each player keeps their own Renown,
and a team's Renown is its players' together: the team reaches the target together and wins together. A team is out
when all its players are out. Legacy Lines stay each player's own.
Holding a Domain in a team game: teammates' Might doesn't add up. A team's Might at a Domain is its strongest
player's there, so a teammate standing with you never takes a Domain from you, and a team holds it as a player would.
For a power that pays "its holder", the teammate with the most Might there is the holder. Every tie rule (your own
Gate, the Crossroads banner, Constantinople, Cleopatra) works side for side.

Targets. 25 Renown for a player on their own at any count (as today); a team: 20 for each player on it and 5 more,
so 45 for a team of two and 65 for a team of three (engine.js targetFor).

Cards whose meaning had to be decided for more players:
  - Columbian Exchange: you choose another player still in the game; your Gate and Outpost change places with theirs.
  - Congress of Vienna: 4 and 5 are the Outpost and Gate of the player on your left. (It could not be played with more
    than two players before.)
  - "Than the other player" counts (Pericles' kind, Gibraltar): every enemy Figure there together.
  - Persepolis takes 1 Spark from each opponent who has Figures there. Lisbon still needs exactly one player there.
  - The Tribune's Veto: a Deed aimed at a Figure can be stopped only by the player whose Figure it is; one that
    doubles Renown by any opponent (the first round the table from the Deed's player's left who has the Veto and
    the Spark kept). A teammate's Veto never plays.
  - Lisbon Earthquake hits every Figure there, teammates' too; Eclipse, Ambush, Canossa and the Figures that stop or
    push "enemy Figures" never reach a teammate's. The Rift can swap with a teammate's Figure.
  - Joan of Arc, Independence Hall, catching up (+1 Spark when behind) and the Bounty compare sides' Renown, and only
    players still in the game. Empress Wu needs more Domains than anyone else.
  - Machu Picchu on the side of a player who is out lets anyone in. The empty Gate of a player who is out is nobody's.
  - The Boundary Stone stops opponents' cards, not a teammate's.

The robots. With two players they judge exactly as before. With more (evalM in engine.js) they weigh each opponent by
how close their side is to the lead (an opponent 8 Renown behind the leading opponent counts a quarter as much), so
they go after the leader; a teammate counts as themselves; a Gate whose player is down to the last Seal counts three
times as much to defend and twice as much to take. Measured against robots that weigh every opponent alike
(mw gap 1000000): the leader after round 4 goes on to win less often (4 players 34.7% against 39.0%; 5 players 25.3%
against 30.3%; 3 players level), and nothing else moves beyond noise.

3. What changes with more players, and what to decide
------------------------------------------------------
3a. Being put out leaves people watching. With the rules as accepted, a player is put out in 70% of 3-player games
and almost every game of 5 or 6, the first in round 5 or 6, and a player put out sits out 0.8 rounds (3 players) to
1.5 rounds (5 and 6 players) of a 7.5-round game: at six players that is about 13 turns of other people's. Measured:

"outEnds": the count that puts a company out ends the game, and the company still in with the most Renown wins (with
two players that is today's rule word for word). Games are shorter and the winner has less Renown (19.6 at 4
players, 16.9 at 6), and the leader after round 4 wins more often (44% and 50%, against 35% and 25%): the game becomes
a race to be ahead when somebody falls. It also brings the raiding decks back (3b).

3b. The raiding decks are weak with more players. Breaking Seals wins a two-player game, but with more players it

3c. Teams and Might. Teammates' Might adding up ('add') against not adding up ('own', the rule): at 2 v 2 (45) teams
51.0 / 49.0 against 54.2 / 45.8, 7.72 rounds against 7.78, 1.10 players put out a game against 1.07;
at 3 v 3 (65) 50.0 / 50.0 against 53.0 / 47.0. Neither is out of line. 'own' is one sentence ("your Might and your
teammate's don't add up; whoever of you has more there holds it for your team") and keeps two teammates from stacking
six Figures on one Domain; 'add' reads as "a team holds a Domain with its Might together". Tim's call.


4. Decisions for Tim
--------------------
  1. Being put out (3a): (a) as accepted: out of the game, the others play on; (b) four Seals with three or more
     players; (c) the game ends at the count that puts a company out, and the company still in with the most Renown
     wins. Recommendation: (c) for 5 and 6 players at least, where (a) has someone sitting out in most games (with
     Normal robots 1.4 to 1.7 players a game, for about a round each: 5 or 6 other people's turns); it is also the rule
     that keeps the raiding decks in the game (3b), and with two players it is today's rule. If Tim keeps (a), (b)
     cuts the waiting by about a third.
  2. What an eliminated player's Domains do (built as recommended): they stay where they lie with their banners, and
     their Gate and Outpost are Domains anyone can hold, that score and break nothing. Removing a spoke would reshape
     the map mid-game and strand Figures standing there.
  3. The target: 25 on your own at every count, 45 for a team of two, 65 for a team of three (20 a player and 5 more).
     Recommendation: as measured; games last as long as two-player games do now.
  4. Teams' Might at a Domain (3c): recommendation 'own' (doesn't add up), the rule built.
  5. Turn order: the leader first and round the table to the left (built). No seat needs a Spark or any other help.
  6. The raiding decks with more players (3b): a deck pass for games of more players, Renown for putting a company out
     (outRen 3 took Pathfinders from 6.8 to 11.3 at 4 players), or decision 1 (c).
  7. The card words in section 5 (they read right at every count and say exactly what two players play today).


5. Card and Domain words that would mislead with more players
--------------------------------------------------------------
The engine already plays each of these the way the new words say (and the new words say exactly what a two-player
game does today, so they could go in now). "Opponent" is a player who isn't you or your teammate; the game's word for
their Figures stays "enemy Figures". Nothing here is changed in text.js yet: these are proposals.

Skills, on every card that has them
  Raider (Boudica, Hannibal, Blackbeard, Ching Shih, Scipio, Bolívar, Amanirenas, Zenobia, Richard, Nanny, Grace
  O'Malley, Thutmose III)
    now:  Raider: +2 Might when attacking on the other player's side.
    new:  Raider: +2 Might when attacking on an opponent's side.
  Pericles, Shaka, Abraham Lincoln, Fu Hao, Napoleon (and the retired Akbar card)
    now:  +2 Might while you have more Figures here than the other player.
    new:  +2 Might while your Figures here outnumber enemy Figures.
          (the engine counts every enemy Figure there together; a teammate's Figures count for neither side)
  Yaa Asantewaa, Voltaire, Mark Twain (and the retired Anansi)
    now:  When you play this Figure, you gain 1 Spark, or 2 if the other player holds this Place.
    new:  When you play this Figure, you gain 1 Spark, or 2 if an opponent holds this Place.

Deeds and Relics
  Phalanx      now:  ... On the other player's turn, Phalanx plays itself when the +2 would save your Figure ...
               new:  ... On an opponent's turn, Phalanx plays itself when the +2 would save your Figure ...
  Testudo      now:  ... loses a clash it started on the other player's side ...
               new:  ... loses a clash it started on an opponent's side ...
  Tribune's Veto  now:  When the other player plays a Deed that ...
                  new:  When an opponent plays a Deed that ... (a Deed aimed at a Figure can be stopped only by
                        the player whose Figure it is; one that doubles Renown by any opponent, the first round the
                        table from its player's left who has the Veto and the Spark kept)
  Pope Leo I   now:  Ward: the other player's Deeds have no effect on your Figures here, ...
               new:  Ward: opponents' Deeds have no effect on your Figures here, ...
  Lisbon Earthquake  now:  Every Figure there, yours and the other player's, misses its next turn.
                     new:  Every Figure there, on every side, misses its next turn.
  Edict of Milan     now:  Until this round ends, the other player can't start a clash with your Figures there.
                     new:  Until this round ends, no opponent can start a clash with your Figures there.
  William the Conqueror  now:  When you play this Figure at the other player's Gate or Outpost, you may discard the
                               Domain there. The other player lays the top Domain of their stack in its place.
                         new:  When you play this Figure at an opponent's Gate or Outpost, you may discard the Domain
                               there. That player lays the top Domain of their stack in its place.
  Earthquake of Rhodes   now:  Choose a Gate or an Outpost, yours or the other player's. ...
                         new:  Choose any player's Gate or Outpost, yours included. ...
  Boundary Stone   now:  The other player's cards can't replace or move the Domains at your Gate and your Outpost, ...
                   new:  Opponents' cards can't replace or move the Domains at your Gate and your Outpost, ...
  Congress of Vienna  now:  ... 4 their Outpost and 5 their Gate ...
                      new:  ... 4 the Outpost of the player on your left and 5 their Gate ...
  The Columbian Exchange  now:  The Domains at the two Gates change places, and the Domains at the two Outposts
                                change places. ...
                          new:  Choose another player. The Domains at your Gate and theirs change places, and so do the
                                Domains at your Outpost and theirs. ...
  Rift: unchanged ("At least one must be yours": the other can be anyone's, a teammate's too).

Guides
  Joan of Arc  now:  While the other player has more Renown than you, ...
               new:  While an opponent has more Renown than you, ...
  Empress Wu   now:  If you hold more Places than the other player when a round ends, ...
               new:  If you hold more Places than anyone else when a round ends, ...
  (Mandela, who has left the roster: "defeated on an opponent's side")

Domain powers (text.js DRULE)
  Gibraltar     new:  When enemy Figures at the Rock of Gibraltar outnumber a player's Figures there, that player's
                      Figures defending in a clash there have +2 Might.
  Orléans       new:  While an opponent holds Orléans, a Figure of yours that starts a clash there has +2 Might.
  Nan Madol     new:  While a player holds Nan Madol, their opponents can't walk Figures out of it.
  Versailles    new:  Whoever has the costliest Figure at Versailles holds it. If there is a tie for the costliest
                      Figure there, Might decides as usual.
  Roman Forum   new:  Whoever has the most Figures at the Roman Forum holds it. If there is a tie for the most,
                      Might decides as usual.
  Forbidden City  new:  Opponents' Deeds have no effect on your Figures at the Forbidden City.
  Independence Hall  new:  When the player who holds Independence Hall has less Renown than an opponent, it scores
                           2 Renown for them.
  Persepolis    new:  When a round ends, the player who holds Persepolis takes 1 Spark from the next turn of each
                      opponent who has Figures there.
  Tordesillas   new:  When the Might at Tordesillas is tied and every player in the tie has Figures there,
                      Tordesillas scores 1 Renown for each of them.
  Strait of Magellan  new:  ... can walk from there straight to another player's Outpost.
  Kitty Hawk    new:  A Figure can walk from Kitty Hawk to any Domain except another player's Gate.
  Machu Picchu  new:  At a Gate or an Outpost, only the player whose side Machu Picchu is on can walk Figures in,
                      while that player is still in the game.
  The rest already read right at any count ("each player who", "only one player", "whoever holds").

For the rules page (RULES in ui.js) and the guide, with teams: "Your teammate is not your opponent: you can't clash
with each other, nothing that hits an opponent or an enemy Figure hits your teammate, and holding your teammate's Gate
breaks none of their Seals. Your team's Renown is your Renown and your teammates' together."
