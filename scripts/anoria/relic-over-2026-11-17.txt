A Relic played over one of your own (2026-11-17)
===============================================

Tim: "in arvela, i should be able to play a relic over an existing relic in play for me".

The rule
--------
  You can have 2 Relics in play at a time, and they have to be Relics with different powers (as before).
  With 2 Relics in play, a new Relic can be played over either of them for its full cost. The Relic underneath goes
  to its player's discard pile, and the new one has its slot.
  A Relic whose power the player has in play can go only over the Relic that has that power (a second copy over the
  first, the Astrolabe over the Compass), so there are still never two Relics with one power.
  With a free slot and no Relic of the same power in play, a Relic is played as always: nothing is covered.

  The move is {t: 'relic', h, over: slot}; a Relic bound for a free slot has no `over`, as before. Its event is
  {t: 'relic', o, c, over, was}. A game saved before this carries on and gets the new moves (the rule only adds moves).
  cfg.relicOver 0 plays the rule before, for test games; cfg.overAll 1 lets the robots weigh every such move.

What the Relic underneath leaves behind
---------------------------------------
Nothing a Relic does is kept with the Relic or with its slot. Every test of a power asks hasFx as it happens, so the
Relic that left stops at once: the hand limit goes back to 8 with the Manuscripts covered, the Rosetta Stone adds
nothing to the line of the Relic played over it, the Locomotive's walk is gone.
What a power has used is its player's, not the Relic's: P.uA (the jump), P.uRw (Railway), P.uRk (Volley) and P.uLc
(Library Cave) for the turn, P.uCy (Homecoming) for the round, P.rb (the Inca Rope Bridge's Seal) for the game. So a
power that works once is not made new by playing its Relic again: a second Rope Bridge over the first mends nothing
more, and a second Locomotive in the turn its player went by rail gives no second walk. This is what the cards say
("once a game", "once a turn") read as the player's, and it leaves nothing to be gained by covering a Relic with its
own twin but a Legacy Line, Deir el-Bahari's Renown or Gutenberg's card, which any Relic played gives.
The one thing a Relic leaves on a Figure is the Stirrup's Charge (f.gar: a Figure that walked this turn can still
clash). It goes with the Stirrup (relicLeft in engine.js); Dunhuang gives its banner's player's Figures the same, and
that stays.
A Legacy Line is read as the Relic is played, before the Relic underneath leaves. So the Sextant played over the
Astrolabe makes its line from the Astrolabe (the card under a Relic in your hand shows the Astrolabe lit as in play,
and the line it draws is the one that scores).

The robots
----------
A robot plays a Relic over one of its own only when the new Relic's power is worth more to it than the power it would
cover (RW in engine.js, a round's worth of each; the Inca Rope Bridge counts ROPE_W more while its Seal is still to
come, so an armed Rope Bridge is not covered and a spent one is the first to go). Whether that is worth the Spark and
the card is then weighed as any move is. So it never covers a Relic with one of the same power, and Easy's unclear
judgment never makes it throw a better Relic away for a lesser one.
