# Anoria: Domains on the table

In Anoria the world remembers whoever stands at its wonders. Each player brings their own to the game, as a stack
of Domain cards that sits beside their deck.

## What a Domain is

A Domain is a real place that people once held in order to hold their world. It lies at one of the five positions
on the table: your Gate, your Outpost, the Crossroads, the other player's Outpost and the other player's Gate.

Every Domain card tells you two things besides its name.

- **Its kind.** The kind is part of what you call the card: a Commerce Domain, a Thought Domain. The kind says what
  sort of place it was, and its rule gives you what holding that sort of place gave people.
- **Its home.** The home is the part of the world the Domain stands in. A Figure from that part of the world is at
  home there and has +1 Might, whichever player brought the Domain and wherever it lies.

| Kind | The places | What it pays in | What holding one gives you |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A Commerce Domain | Markets, ports and caravan cities | Spark | You get 1 more Spark next turn, or the first Figure you lay there each turn costs 1 less. |
| An Industry Domain | Workshops, shipyards, mines and kilns | Made things | Your Relics and Deeds cost 1 less, or a Figure has +1 Might on the turn it arrives there. |
| A Thought Domain | Libraries, schools and observatories | Cards | You draw a card each round, or a Legacy Line made there scores 1 more Renown. |
| A Land Domain | Walls, fortresses and passes | Safe ground | Figures defending there get +1 Might, or only the player whose side it lies on can walk Figures in, or each player can have no more than two Figures there. |
| A Culture Domain | Theatres, temples, monuments and games | Renown | The Domain scores 2 Renown when you hold it with two Figures, or winning a clash there scores 1 Renown. |
| A Law Domain | Capitals, courts and council fires | Order | Only the player who holds it can start a clash there, or the other player's Deeds cannot target your Figures there. |
| A Routes Domain | Roads, straits and sea lanes | Movement | A Figure can walk between that Domain and any other, or a Figure can walk on the turn it arrives there. |

A few Domains give +1 Might to the Figures whose world they were: Arts Figures at a theatre, Discovery Figures at an
observatory, Statecraft Figures at a capital. Each Domain card says its own rule in one sentence. A Commerce Domain
pays at an Outpost or at the Crossroads. It pays nothing at a Gate.

## Your two stacks

You bring two stacks of cards to the table. Your deck is 40 cards with the navy back. Your Domain stack is 15 Domains
with the same back in oxblood red, so you can tell the two stacks apart at a glance. A Domain stack can hold no more
than one of each Domain. It can hold Domains of any kinds and any homes, so you can fill it with the Domains that
suit your deck best. You choose your 15 from all the Domains you own. You and the other player can both have the
same Domain in your stacks.

## What is in the box

Each player has:

- a deck of 40 cards and a Domain stack of 15
- a Guide card and a play mat
- five banners in their own colour
- three Seals, a Renown marker and seven Spark counters
- one Legacy Line counter, which is turned over when a Legacy Line has scored this round

The two players share:

- the round track, numbered 1 to 14, with the Renown track from 0 to 25 along its edge
- the round marker and the first-turn marker
- two dice
- three Silence markers
- twelve +1 Might markers
- six hourglass counters

## Setting up

1. Lay the two play mats so that they meet in a seam, and lay the round track beside the seam with the round marker
   on 1.
2. Shuffle your Domain stack and draw three Domains. Look at them without showing them.
3. Lay one of them face down at your Gate and one face down at your Outpost. Keep the third as your offer for the
   Crossroads.
4. When both of you have done this, turn your Gates and Outposts face up, and put one of your banners on each.
5. Each of you rolls a die, and you roll again if the two dice match. The player with the higher roll has first
   dibs: that player's third Domain is laid at the Crossroads with one of their banners on it.
6. The other player puts their third Domain face up beside their stack, where it starts their Domain discard pile.
   That player takes the first-turn marker and the first turn of the game.

A Domain can lie on the table only once. If you turn up a Domain that is already on the table, put it under your
stack and turn up the next one. If the two of you turn up the same Domain in step 4, each of you puts your copy under
your stack and lays the next Domain of your stack in its place.

## Whose Domain is whose

A banner in your colour lies on every Domain you brought. The banner stays on the card wherever the card goes, and
it comes back to you when the card is discarded.

Your Gate and your Outpost are positions, and the positions are always yours. A card can move a Domain, so the
Domain that lies at your Gate may have the other player's banner on it. The position decides these things:

- If the other player holds your Gate when a round ends, one of your Seals breaks.
- You hold your own Gate when the Might there is tied, and when nobody stands there.
- A rule or a card that names a Gate, an Outpost or a side means the position.

The banner decides these two things:

- If the Might at the Crossroads is tied, and the player whose banner is on the Domain there has a Figure there,
  that player holds the Crossroads.
- A Domain that is discarded goes to the Domain discard pile of the player whose banner is on it.

A Domain's rule and its home are the same for both players, whoever brought it.

## When a Domain is replaced

Some cards replace a Domain. The Domain that was there goes face up on the Domain discard pile of the player whose
banner is on it, and the banner goes back to that player. The new Domain is laid in the same position, with a banner
of the player who brought it.

- The Figures standing at that position stay where they are.
- A Royal Decree played there this round still counts, and Imhotep keeps the Might he has earned by staying there.
- The new Domain's rule works from that moment. A rule that pays at the end of the round pays for the Domain that is
  lying there when the round ends.
- If Vesuvius was lying on the old Domain, Vesuvius is discarded with it.

If your Domain stack runs out, shuffle your Domain discard pile and make it your stack again.

## When a Domain moves

Some cards move Domains from one position to another. Pick up each Domain that moves, with its banner and anything
lying on it, and lay it in its new position.

- The Figures stay at the positions where they stand, and so does a Royal Decree.
- The Domain keeps its rule and its home. A Commerce Domain that arrives at a Gate pays nothing while it lies there.
- If Diogenes stands at one of the positions, his Silence marker covers the Domain that lies under him now.

## Markers

- **A Domain with no rule.** Vesuvius is its own marker: the card lies across the Domain's rule, goes wherever the
  Domain goes and is discarded with it. Diogenes silences the Domain he stands on, so the player who plays him lays
  a Silence marker on that Domain's rule. The marker belongs to Diogenes. It comes off when he walks away or is
  knocked out, and if the Domain under him moves, the marker stays with him and covers the Domain that arrives.
- **Might that lasts a while.** A card that adds Might lies where it works until it stops working. A Royal Decree
  lies beside the Domain on your side until the round ends. Phalanx lies under the Figure it helps until the clash
  is over. A Figure whose Might has grown for longer than that carries +1 Might markers: Imhotep takes one at the
  end of each round he has stayed where he is, up to two, and a Figure on a Legacy Line carries one for as long as
  the Figure who inspired them is in play.
- **A Figure that has acted, or that must miss a turn,** is turned sideways until its owner's next turn.

## Rounds and turns

A round is one turn for each player. The round track counts the rounds, and each square shows the Spark a player
starts a turn with in that round: 2 in round 1 and 1 more each round, up to 7.

- The player with more Renown takes the first turn of a round and holds the first-turn marker. If Renown is level,
  the player who took the first turn of the game goes first in odd rounds and the other player goes first in even
  rounds. The squares of the track are marked to show which.
- When both players have taken their turn, count the Renown for every Domain held, break a Seal for a Gate that the
  other player holds, and pay the rules that pay at the end of a round. Then the player who took the second turn
  moves the round marker on one square, turns both Legacy Line counters face up again and takes away any Royal
  Decree.
- Round 5 is marked for Abraham Lincoln, who mends one broken Seal when it begins. Round 14 is the last round.
- A card whose effect lasts a number of rounds gets that many hourglass counters when it is played. The player who
  moves the round marker takes one counter off each such card, and the effect ends when the last counter comes off.

## The cards that change Domains

These cards go in your deck of 40 like any other card.

| Card | Kind and cost | What the card says |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pachacuti | Figure, costs 4, 4 Might | When Pachacuti arrives, look at the top three Domains of your stack. You may lay one of them at your Gate or your Outpost in place of the Domain that is there. Put the others under your stack. |
| Al-Idrisi | Figure, costs 2, 3 Might | When Al-Idrisi arrives, you may look through your whole Domain stack, choose one Domain and lay it at your Gate or your Outpost in place of the Domain that is there. Then shuffle your stack. |
| Christopher Wren | Figure, costs 2, 3 Might | When Christopher Wren arrives, you may take a Domain from your Domain discard pile and lay it at your Gate or your Outpost in place of the Domain that is there. |
| Constantine | Figure, costs 5, 5 Might | When Constantine arrives, you may turn up the top Domain of your stack and lay it at the Crossroads in place of the Domain that is there. |
| William the Conqueror | Figure, costs 5, 5 Might | When William arrives at the other player's Gate or Outpost, you may have the Domain there discarded. The other player lays the top Domain of their stack in its place. |
| Diogenes | Figure, costs 1, 2 Might | The Domain where Diogenes stands has no rule. |
| Earthquake | Deed, costs 2 | Choose a Gate or an Outpost, yours or the other player's. The Domain there is discarded, and the player whose side it is on lays the top Domain of their stack in its place. |
| Vesuvius | Deed, costs 1 | Lay Vesuvius on a Domain. That Domain has no rule for as long as Vesuvius lies on it. Vesuvius goes wherever the Domain goes, and is discarded with it. |
| The Cape Route | Deed, costs 2 | Turn up the top Domain of your stack and lay it at the Crossroads in place of the Domain that is there. |
| Treaty of Breda | Deed, costs 2 | Choose two Domains. They change places. |
| The Columbian Exchange | Deed, costs 3 | The Domains at the two Gates change places, and the Domains at the two Outposts change places. |
| Boundary Stone | Relic, costs 1 | The other player's cards cannot replace, move or silence the Domains at your Gate and your Outpost. |
