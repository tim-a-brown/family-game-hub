# Anoria: Places on the table

In Anoria the world remembers whoever stands at its wonders. Each player brings their own wonders to the game, as a
stack of Place cards that sits beside their deck.

## What a Place is

Every Place is a real place that people once held in order to hold their world. A Place's **domain** says what kind
of place it was, and its rule gives you what holding that kind of place gave them.

| Domain | The Places | What it pays in | What holding one gives you |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Commerce | Markets, ports and caravan cities | Spark | You get 1 more Spark next turn, or your Figures arrive there for 1 less. |
| Industry | Workshops, shipyards, mines and kilns | Made things | Your Relics and Deeds cost 1 less, or a Figure has +1 Might on the turn it arrives there. |
| Ideas | Libraries, schools and observatories | Cards | You draw a card each round, or a Legacy Line made there scores 1 more Renown. |
| Land | Walls, fortresses and passes | Safe ground | Figures defending there get +1 Might, or only you can walk Figures in, or each player can have no more than two Figures there. |
| Culture | Theatres, temples, monuments and games | Renown | The Place scores 2 Renown when you hold it with two Figures, or winning a clash there scores 1 Renown. |
| Law | Capitals, courts and council fires | Safety | Your last Seal cannot be broken while you hold the Place, or the other player's Deeds cannot target your Figures there. |
| Routes | Roads, straits and sea lanes | Movement | The Place is linked to every other Place, or a Figure can walk on the turn it arrives there. |

A few Places give +1 Might to the Figures whose world they were: Arts Figures at a theatre, Discovery Figures at an
observatory, Statecraft Figures at a capital.

Each Place card says its own rule in one sentence. A Commerce Place pays at an Outpost or at the Crossroads. It pays
nothing at a Gate.

## Why holding several domains matters

People who held the markets, the workshops, the schools, the strongholds, the stages, the courts and the roads of
their age held the age. In the game, each domain you hold pays you in a different way, so a player who holds three
of them has more Spark, more cards and more Renown than a player who holds one.

**Domination.** If four or more different domains lie on the table when a round ends, and you hold a Place of every
one of them, you win the game.

## Your two stacks

You bring two stacks of cards to the table. Your deck is 40 cards with the navy back. Your Place stack is 12 Places
with the same back in oxblood red, so you can tell the two stacks apart at a glance. A Place stack can hold no more
than one of each Place. You and the other player can both have the same Place in your stacks.

## Setting up

1. Shuffle your Place stack and draw three Places. Look at them without showing them.
2. Lay one of them face down at your Gate and one face down at your Outpost. Keep the third as your offer for the
   Crossroads.
3. When both of you have done this, turn your Gates and Outposts face up.
4. Each of you rolls a die, and you roll again if the two dice match. The player with the higher roll has first
   dibs: that player's third Place is laid at the Crossroads.
5. The other player puts their third Place face up beside their stack, where it starts their Place discard pile.
   That player takes the first turn of the game.

A Place can lie on the table only once. If you turn up a Place that is already on the table, put it under your stack
and turn up the next one.

## Whose Place the Crossroads is

Your Gate and your Outpost are always your own cards. The Crossroads is the only Place that can be either player's.
The Crossroads card lies on its owner's side of the seam where the two mats meet, turned to face its owner, so both
of you can always see whose card it is.

If the Might at the Crossroads is tied and the player whose Place it is has a Figure there, that player holds the
Crossroads. That is the only thing the owner gets. The Place's rule works for both of you.

## When a Place is replaced

Some cards make a player replace a Place. The Place that was there goes face up on its owner's Place discard pile,
and the new Place is laid in the same spot.

- The Figures standing at that spot stay where they are.
- A Royal Decree played there this round still counts, and Imhotep keeps the Might he has earned by staying there.
- The new Place's rule works from that moment. A rule that pays at the end of the round pays for the Place that is
  lying there when the round ends.
- If Vesuvius was lying on the old Place, Vesuvius is discarded.
- A new Place at the Crossroads lies on the side of the player whose stack it came from.

If your Place stack runs out, shuffle your Place discard pile and make it your stack again.

## The cards that change Places

These ten cards go in your deck of 40 like any other card.

| Card | Kind and cost | What the card says |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pachacuti | Figure, costs 4, 4 Might | When Pachacuti arrives, look at the top three Places of your stack. You may lay one of them at your Gate or your Outpost in place of the Place that is there. Put the others under your stack. |
| Al-Idrisi | Figure, costs 2, 3 Might | When Al-Idrisi arrives, you may look through your whole Place stack, choose one Place and lay it at your Gate or your Outpost in place of the Place that is there. Then shuffle your stack. |
| Christopher Wren | Figure, costs 2, 3 Might | When Christopher Wren arrives, you may take a Place from your Place discard pile and lay it at your Gate or your Outpost in place of the Place that is there. |
| Constantine | Figure, costs 5, 5 Might | When Constantine arrives, you may turn up the top Place of your stack and lay it at the Crossroads in place of the Place that is there. |
| William the Conqueror | Figure, costs 5, 5 Might | When William arrives at the other player's Gate or Outpost, you may make them replace that Place with the top Place of their stack. |
| Diogenes | Figure, costs 1, 2 Might | The Place where Diogenes stands has no rule. |
| Earthquake | Deed, costs 2 | Choose a Gate or an Outpost, yours or the other player's. Its owner replaces that Place with the top Place of their stack. |
| Vesuvius | Deed, costs 1 | Lay Vesuvius on a Place. That Place has no rule for as long as Vesuvius lies on it. |
| The Cape Route | Deed, costs 2 | Turn up the top Place of your stack and lay it at the Crossroads in place of the Place that is there. |
| Boundary Stone | Relic, costs 1 | The other player's cards cannot replace your Gate or your Outpost, and cannot take away their rules. |
