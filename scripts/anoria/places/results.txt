ANORIA: DOMAINS AS EACH PLAYER'S OWN STACK. Test games, 2026-11-11.
scripts/anoria/places/run-places.js, on engine-places.js (a copy of the game's engine: with no stacks it plays the
same games as games/anoria/engine.js, checked game for game).

THE WORDS
  A Domain is the card that lies at a Gate, an Outpost or the Crossroads (it was "Place", "wonder" and "location").
  Its kind is part of its name: a Commerce, Industry, Thought, Land, Culture, Law or Routes Domain ("Ideas" is now
  Thought). Its home is the region it stands in. Gate, Outpost and Crossroads are the three positions.
  "Today" in these tables is the game as it stands, with its shared set of 12 Places.

HOW THE GAMES WERE PLAYED
  Every game is first to 25 Renown or three Seals, with the game's other rules as they stand (STD in engine.js).
  A cell is the five ready decks (40 cards) against each other, all 20 pairings, both seats, each deck played in its
  own style, as scripts/anoria/variant.js does. 2,000 games a cell unless a table says otherwise.
  Noise: on 2,000 games a share is good to about 1.1 points either way, on 1,200 to about 1.4. One deck's win rate
  (800 of 2,000 games) is good to about 1.8. The spread between the five decks moves by chance alone: today's game
  measures 5.6 on one set of 2,000 games and 7.5 on another. Read a spread under 8 as "about the same as today".
  The robots choose by Might, Renown, Seals and a rough value for each rule. They are steady, not clever: a number
  here is a comparison between rules, not a forecast of what people will do.
  Part A is the second round of tests: stacks of 15 from 75 Domains, on the game's cards as they are after the
  legends left the set, with Domination left out. Part B is the first round (stacks of 12 from 60, the cards as they
  were that morning): the tables that settled set-up, the Crossroads edge and the first ten cards.

THE RULES AS THEY NOW STAND (RULES.md is the sheet for the table)
  - Each player brings a stack of 15 Domains, no two the same, chosen from all they own. The same Domain may be in
    both players' stacks, but it lies on the table only once.
  - Set-up: draw three, lay one at your Gate and one at your Outpost, offer the third for the Crossroads. Each player
    rolls a die: the higher roll's offer is the Crossroads, and the other player takes the first turn.
  - A banner in a player's colour lies on every Domain that player brought, and travels with it. If the Might at the
    Crossroads is tied and the player whose banner is there has a Figure there, that player holds it.
  - A Domain that is replaced goes to the discard pile of the player whose banner is on it. Figures, a Royal Decree
    and Imhotep's count stay at the position. An empty stack is made again from the discard pile.
  - A Commerce Domain pays at an Outpost or the Crossroads, not at a Gate, and makes one Figure a turn cheaper.
    At a Law Domain only the player who holds it can start a clash.

  What it says.
  - A Renown bonus for three kinds is a win-more rule. Your own Gate and Outpost are two kinds already, so the
    bonus goes to whoever holds the Crossroads as well: four fifths of it to the player already ahead. Games get half
    a round shorter and the winning margin a point wider. Not recommended.
  - The same bonus at four kinds hardly ever pays (once in three games) and nearly always to the leader. Nothing.
  - Doubling a kind's rules when you hold two Domains of it changes nothing measurable, and it can't be said simply:
    a rule that gives Might decides who holds the Domain, which decides whether the rule is doubled. Not recommended.
  - Winning by holding every kind on the table is too sudden when any table counts: 40% of games, some of them over
    in round 3 because only three kinds were dealt.
  - With four or more kinds on the table it is a third way to win in about one game in seven. It asks for four
    Domains, one of them on the other player's side. It takes its wins from games the leader was already winning
    (98%), so it changes who wins very little and ends won games a round sooner. It is the one rule of the six that
    makes the kinds matter on the table without rewarding the leader with still more Renown. Recommended, as
    "Domination", with that caution: it is a finish, not a comeback.
  The kinds matter most through their own rules, which is where the test games put the weight: each kind pays in
  a different coin (Spark, cheaper cards, cards, safe ground, Renown, a safe Seal, movement), so holding three
  different ones compounds in a way holding three of one does not.
